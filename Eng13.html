“Happy birthday,” “selamat hari jadi,” or “sanah helwah” have become customary greetings around the world. It is rare to come across someone who does not celebrate their birthday; perhaps they are antisocial. But do you know how this tradition began? A wise person would certainly not simply follow a practice without taking the time to learn about the origins of the celebration they observe. Therefore, let us take a journey through the history of birthdays.

Some say that the celebration of birthdays was started by Pharaoh, based on Genesis 40:20 in the Bible. No, it actually dates back much further—around 4,000 years ago in ancient Sumer.

Professor Vladimir V. Emelianov, a scholar of history at Saint Petersburg State University, in his 2017 journal article entitled “First Account of a Birthday in Human History”, states that the first person known to have celebrated a birthday was a Sumerian king named Lugalanda. Five discovered Sumerian administrative tablets record that Lugalanda celebrated the birthdays of his children by preparing various kinds of lavish food as offerings to the idol of the goddess Bau. He did so to display his royal lineage.
